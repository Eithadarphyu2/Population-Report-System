# Code of Conduct & Team Ethics

**Team:** Team 14  
**Project:** Population Reporting System

## 1. Preamble & Purpose

This Code of Conduct defines the standards of behaviour, collaboration,
communication, contribution, and ethical working expected from every member
of Team 14.

Our goal is to create a respectful, supportive, fair, and productive team
environment where every member can contribute to the project, learn from
others, and take responsibility for their work.

This document provides the principles and rules that will guide how we
communicate, develop, test, review, and manage our project throughout the
coursework.

The purpose of this Code of Conduct is not to punish team members unfairly.
It is to encourage accountability, fair contribution, transparency, and
professional teamwork.

---

## 2. Our Core Values

The following values guide our team's behaviour and working practices:

- **Respect:** We respect each member's opinions, time, skills, and
  responsibilities.

- **Integrity:** We communicate honestly and do not misrepresent our work,
  progress, or contributions.

- **Collaboration:** We work together, share knowledge, and help each other
  solve problems.

- **Responsibility:** Each member takes responsibility for their assigned
  tasks and contributes to the overall success of the project.

- **Quality:** We aim to produce reliable, understandable, and maintainable
  code and documentation.

- **Fair Contribution:** Every member should make meaningful contributions
  to development, testing, reviews, and Scrum activities.

- **Continuous Learning:** We support each other in learning new concepts
  and improving our technical and teamwork skills.

- **Accountability:** Each member is responsible for completing agreed work
  and communicating honestly when difficulties occur.

---

## 3. Code of Ethics

As a member of Team 14, I will:

### 3.1 Be Honest About My Work

I will accurately represent the work I have completed. I will not claim
another member's work as my own or make false claims about my contribution.

### 3.2 Give Credit to Others

I will acknowledge the work and ideas contributed by other team members.
I will not take credit for another member's code, documentation, ideas,
or other project contributions.

### 3.3 Take Responsibility

I will take responsibility for my assigned tasks and my decisions. If I
make a mistake, I will communicate it to the team and work towards a
solution rather than hiding the problem.

### 3.4 Protect Project Information

I will handle project information responsibly and will not intentionally
share private team discussions, credentials, access information, or other
sensitive project information outside the team.

### 3.5 Use Development Tools Responsibly

I will use GitHub, IntelliJ IDEA, Maven, Docker, and other project tools
responsibly and follow the team's agreed development practices.

### 3.6 Maintain Academic Integrity

I will not deliberately present another person's work as my own.

Where external resources, examples, or assistance are used, I will follow
the relevant academic and coursework requirements for acknowledging or
documenting their use.

## 4. Rules of Communication

### 4.1 Respectful Communication

All communication through meetings, group chats, GitHub, or other agreed
channels should be respectful and constructive.

Personal attacks, insulting language, discrimination, intimidation, or
deliberate humiliation of another member are not acceptable.

### 4.2 Clear Communication

Team members should communicate important information clearly, especially
when discussing:

- Task progress
- Problems or blockers
- Changes to requirements
- Changes to code
- Testing problems
- Deadlines
- Meetings
- GitHub issues and pull requests

### 4.3 Responsiveness

Team members should regularly check the team's agreed communication
channels and respond to important project messages within a reasonable
period.

If a member cannot respond or participate, they should inform the team
when reasonably possible.

### 4.4 Active Listening

During meetings and discussions, team members should listen to each other,
avoid unnecessary interruptions, and allow others to explain their ideas
before responding.

---

## 5. Meetings

### 5.1 Punctuality

Team members should attend agreed meetings on time.

If a member cannot attend or expects to be late, they should inform the
team as early as reasonably possible.

### 5.2 Preparation

Members should prepare for meetings by reviewing relevant tasks,
requirements, code, or other project information when necessary.

### 5.3 Participation

Every member is expected to participate in Scrum activities and contribute
ideas, questions, progress updates, and discussions.

### 5.4 Meeting Records

Important decisions, task assignments, and agreed actions should be
recorded where appropriate so that the team has a shared record of its
decisions and responsibilities.

---

## 6. Development and GitHub Practices

### 6.1 Shared Development Standards

The team will agree on common coding practices and project structure so
that code written by different members can work together consistently.

### 6.2 Branching

Team members should use the agreed Git branching structure for development.

Feature work should normally be developed on feature branches rather than
directly on the `master` branch.

### 6.3 Meaningful Commits

Members should make clear and meaningful commits that describe the work
being completed.

Commit messages should help the team understand what changed.

### 6.4 Issues and Tasks

Team members should keep their GitHub Issues and assigned tasks updated.

When work is completed, the relevant issue or task should be updated so
that the team's progress can be tracked.

### 6.5 Pull Requests and Reviews

Where pull requests and code reviews are required, members should review
code constructively and respond to review comments appropriately.

Code review should focus on improving the project rather than criticising
the person who wrote the code.

### 6.6 Evidence of Contribution

Team members should maintain appropriate evidence of their contribution,
such as:

- GitHub commits
- Pull requests
- Code reviews
- GitHub Issues
- Task completion
- Testing
- Documentation
- Scrum activities
- Other agreed project work

This evidence may be used when reviewing team contribution.

---

## 7. Work and Collaboration

### 7.1 Reliability

Members should make a reasonable effort to complete tasks they have
agreed to undertake.

If a member expects a task to be delayed, they should inform the team
rather than waiting until the deadline.

### 7.2 Transparency

Members should be open about their progress, difficulties, and blockers.

Team members should not silently leave problems unresolved when asking for
help could allow the team to move forward.

### 7.3 Knowledge Sharing

Members are encouraged to share useful information, explanations,
resources, and technical knowledge with the rest of the team.

No member should intentionally prevent another member from understanding
the part of the project they are working on.

### 7.4 Constructive Feedback

Feedback should be:

- Respectful
- Specific
- Useful
- Focused on the work

Team members should be willing to receive feedback and make improvements
where appropriate.

### 7.5 Helping Other Members

Team members are encouraged to help one another when someone is blocked
or does not understand part of the project.

Helping another member does not remove that member's responsibility for
understanding and contributing to their assigned work.

---

## 8. Unacceptable Behaviour

The following behaviours are not acceptable within Team 14:

- Bullying or intimidation
- Personal attacks or harassment
- Discrimination
- Deliberately humiliating another team member
- Taking credit for another person's work
- Deliberately hiding important project information
- Intentionally disrupting another member's work
- Deliberately damaging project code or GitHub resources
- Repeatedly ignoring agreed team responsibilities without communication
- Repeatedly failing to complete agreed tasks without reasonable explanation
- Falsifying or misrepresenting project contribution
- Deliberately interfering with another member's GitHub work
- Disrespectful behaviour during meetings, discussions, or code reviews

---

# 9. Conflict Resolution

Disagreements are a normal part of teamwork. Team 14 will use the following
process to resolve conflicts:

### Step 1: Direct Discussion

The members involved should first discuss the problem calmly and
respectfully and try to reach an agreement.

### Step 2: Team Discussion

If the issue cannot be resolved directly, the matter can be discussed
with the wider team so that different viewpoints can be considered.

### Step 3: Scrum Master Support

If the team cannot resolve the issue, the Scrum Master will help facilitate
the discussion and work towards a fair resolution.

The Scrum Master should remain neutral when facilitating a disagreement and
should not make an unfair decision in favour of a particular member.

### Step 4: Escalation

If a serious issue remains unresolved, or if it affects the coursework or
team's ability to work, the matter may be raised with the module teaching
team.

# 10. Contribution, Accountability & Fine System

## 10.1 Purpose

Team 14 uses an internal contribution and accountability system to
encourage fair participation and responsibility.

The system is intended to:

- Encourage every member to contribute meaningfully.
- Recognise responsibility for assigned work.
- Discourage repeated failure to meet agreed responsibilities.
- Provide a transparent record of serious contribution problems.
- Encourage members to correct problems rather than simply receive a
  penalty.

The system must be applied fairly and consistently to all members,
regardless of their role.

---

## 10.2 Fine Levels

The team uses four internal fine levels.

| Level | Description | Example | Internal Deduction / Fine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1 – Minor** | A small failure to follow an agreed team rule | Late response, minor missed update, or small task delay without communication | **Buy/bring coffee for the team** |
| **Level 2 – Moderate** | A repeated or more significant failure to meet an agreed responsibility | Repeatedly missing agreed meetings, failing to update assigned work, or leaving a task incomplete without communication | **−0.3 point** |
| **Level 3 – Serious** | A significant failure that affects other team members or project progress | Repeatedly failing to contribute, abandoning assigned work, or repeatedly ignoring agreed responsibilities | **−0.5 point** |
| **Level 4 – Major** | Serious misconduct or deliberate harmful behaviour | Deliberately damaging project resources, deliberately misrepresenting contribution, or intentionally taking another member's work as their own | **−1 point** |

The deduction should be proportionate to the behaviour.

The team should not apply a higher-level deduction when a lower-level
response is appropriate.

---

## 10.3 Corrective Action

A fine should not be the only response.

Where appropriate, the member may also be required to take corrective
action, such as:

- Completing the missed task.
- Updating the relevant GitHub Issue.
- Completing an additional agreed task.
- Participating in a code review.
- Completing required testing.
- Providing missing documentation.
- Explaining the issue during the next team meeting.
- Taking another reasonable action agreed by the team.

The purpose of corrective action is to restore the member's contribution
and help the project continue successfully.

---

## 10.4 Procedure Before Applying a Deduction

A contribution deduction should follow this process:

### Step 1: Identify the Issue

A team member or Scrum Master identifies a possible violation of the
Code of Conduct.

### Step 2: Collect Evidence

The team reviews relevant evidence, such as GitHub activity, task records,
messages, meeting records, or other project evidence.

### Step 3: Discuss the Situation

The member involved is informed of the concern and is given an opportunity
to explain the situation.

### Step 4: Determine the Fine Level

The team considers the seriousness and circumstances of the issue and
agrees on an appropriate fine level.

### Step 5: Record the Decision

The reason, evidence, fine level, deduction, and corrective action should
be recorded.

### Step 6: Complete Corrective Action

The member completes the agreed corrective action where applicable.

---

## 10.5 Exceptional Circumstances

A member should not automatically receive a deduction when circumstances
outside their reasonable control prevent them from completing work.

Examples may include:

- Serious personal circumstances.
- Technical problems that could not reasonably be avoided.
- University commitments.
- Unexpected emergencies.
- Other reasonable circumstances communicated to the team.

The team should consider the circumstances before applying any deduction.

---

## 10.6 Review and Appeal

A member may request a review if they believe a deduction is incorrect,
unfair, or based on incomplete information.

The review should:

1. Reconsider the evidence.
2. Allow the member to explain their position.
3. Consider any relevant circumstances.
4. Confirm, reduce, remove, or otherwise revise the internal deduction
   where appropriate.

If the team cannot reach a fair agreement, the matter may be discussed
with the module teaching team.

# 11. Team Roles and Responsibilities

### Product Owner

The Product Owner is responsible for:

- Helping define and prioritise the Product Backlog.
- Clarifying requirements and user stories.
- Supporting acceptance of completed work.
- Communicating the intended product value to the team.
- Working with the team to maintain a clear project direction.

### Scrum Master

The Scrum Master is responsible for:

- Supporting the Scrum process.
- Helping organise Scrum activities.
- Identifying and helping remove blockers.
- Supporting communication within the team.
- Helping the team follow the agreed working process.
- Facilitating conflict resolution where necessary.

The Scrum Master is also a contributing team member and should complete
agreed project work.

### Team Members

All team members are responsible for:

- Completing agreed tasks.
- Contributing to development, testing, documentation, reviews, and
  project activities.
- Communicating blockers.
- Following GitHub and branching practices.
- Supporting other members.
- Participating in Scrum activities.
- Maintaining evidence of their contribution.

---

# 12. Changes to This Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct may be updated when the team agrees that a change is
necessary.

Any significant change should be discussed with the team before being
adopted.

Changes should be communicated clearly to all six members.

The contribution and fine system should not be changed retrospectively to
penalise a member for behaviour that occurred before the change.
